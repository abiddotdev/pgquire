Sample databases

Databases you can open from a link

Each of these is a whole Postgres database, tables, rows, saved queries and extensions, packed into one link. Click one and it opens in pgquire, running in your browser. Nothing to install, no account, and no server holds the data.

1The link is the database

Everything after the # is the data, compressed. Browsers never send that part to any server.

2pgquire asks first

It shows what's in the link and adds it as a new database in your browser only when you say so.

3It's yours to change

It's a copy. Edit rows, run the saved queries, break things. The link stays as it was.

crm

A small CRM

A sales team's database: 24 companies, their contacts, 140 deals moving from lead to won or lost, and every call, email and demo along the way. The saved queries answer the questions a sales lead asks on Monday morning. Open the ERD to see how it fits together, or edit a deal and watch the pipeline change.

Tables

  • activities 560 rows
  • companies 24 rows
  • contacts 96 rows
  • deals 140 rows

Saved queries

  • Pipeline by stage
  • Won revenue by month
  • Top accounts
  • Stale deals (no touch in 30 days)
  • Win rate by owner

field-notes

Bird sightings, with pg_trgm

A season of birdwatching notes, typed in a hurry: "robbin", "Blue Jaay", just "cardinal". Next to them, a clean list of 30 species. The link brings the pg_trgm extension with it, so Postgres can find a bird however it was spelled and match each messy note to the right species. A GIN index keeps the search fast.

Tables

  • sightings 420 rows
  • species 30 rows

Saved queries

  • Search, typos and all: robbin
  • Match each entry to a species
  • Most seen species, after matching
  • How similar are these?

Extension

  • pg_trgm, added by the link

city-bikes · the big one

City bike trips, the big one

A month of a city bike scheme: 180,000 trips between 80 stations, with who rode, for how long, and on which bike. It's here to show how much a link can hold: every row is inside it, and your browser runs real Postgres over all of them. Find the busiest stations, the rush hours, and the routes people ride most.

Tables

  • stations 80 rows
  • trips 180,000 rows

Saved queries

  • Busiest stations
  • Trips by hour of day
  • Members and casual riders
  • Top routes
  • Busiest day
Open in pgquire 11.4 MB of SQL in a 1.7 MB link
